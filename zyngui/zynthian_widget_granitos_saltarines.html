Granitos saltarines

A granular delay. The input is recorded into a 3 s buffer, and the sound comes back as many short grains read from it. A bouncing ball makes the grains hop between positions in the buffer, which gives the plugin its character.

Widget

The strip is the delay buffer, a ring of 3 s: the sound is recorded moving along it and wraps around at the end, so near the end of the strip some grains can already be back at the start.

  • The thin line is where the grains read by default: the delay time behind the recording point.
  • Each dot is a grain that is sounding, drawn where it really is reading (up to 16 dots). The size of a dot is the level of its grain: tiny when the grain starts and ends, biggest in the middle of its life.
  • The ball bounces above the strip all the time: it settles, rests for a moment and is thrown again. Its size follows Hop: the bigger the ball, the harder it pulls the grains. While it is high and Hop is up, new grains start closer to the present, so the dots jump away from the line.
  • The bar under the strip shows how many grains sound at once, 2 grains per segment.

Delay

  • Time: how far back in the buffer the grains start, in ms.
  • Feedback: how much of the output goes back into the buffer, so each repeat is heard again. High values give long, building repeats; they are softly limited, so they never run away.
  • Mix: dry signal against the granular (wet) signal.
  • Bypass: passes the input through untouched, without clicks. While bypassed the plugin does no work and the buffer is cleared, so the delay starts empty when you switch it back on.

Grains

  • Grain size: length of each grain, in ms. Short grains sound grainy, long ones sound smooth.
  • Density: how many grains start per second, with the spacing between them randomised by up to ±50 %. The number of grains sounding at once is about Density × Grain size: 1 at the defaults, around 25 at 50 grains/s with 500 ms grains.
  • Jitter: randomness of each grain's start position and pitch. At 100 % the pitch of a grain can wander up to an octave either way.

Hop

  • Hop: how far the grains follow the bouncing ball. While the ball is high, new grains start closer to the present, so the echoes jump with the ball: by up to half the delay time at 100 %. At 0 the ball still bounces but the grains ignore it. This is the main character control.
  • Gravity: how quickly the ball settles. At 0 it keeps bouncing for about 6 seconds, at 100 it settles in about a second. You see it in the ball, and hear it when Hop is up.
  • Freeze: stops recording new input, so the buffer keeps looping the same material and the grains keep hopping over it. Nothing new is written while frozen, so Feedback has no effect either.

Variation

  • Pitch: transposes the grains, in semitones.
  • Reverse: the chance that a grain plays backwards.
  • Stereo spread: how far each grain's random pan reaches from the centre: 0 keeps everything in the middle, 100 spreads the grains across the whole stereo field.

Tips

Raise Hop slowly from 0 to hear the echoes start to jump: high values can sound chaotic. Lower Gravity for a ball that keeps the echoes jumping for longer, raise it for a short burst of jumps each time the ball is thrown. Try a long grain size with low density for smooth, sustained textures, and short grains with high density for clouds. Turn Freeze on to play with the hop over a captured moment.
